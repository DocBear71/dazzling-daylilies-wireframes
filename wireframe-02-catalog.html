Page Annotations: Catalog Page (Wireframe 02 of 17)

Notes for Jean and Edward. Plain English explanations of what each region does and what decisions this layout commits us to.

Page Title and Result Count

"Daylilies" is the page heading. The result count on the right ("Showing 1-12 of 48 plants") updates automatically as customers apply filters or search. If filters narrow the results all the way to zero, the grid shows a message like "No plants match your current filters" with a link to clear them and start over.

Search Box

Customers type a plant name and the grid filters in real time. Typing "sunset" would immediately show only plants with "sunset" in the name. This wireframe shows it searching by name only, which is the most predictable behavior for plant browsers.

Decision needed: Should search also match color, hybridizer name, or description text? Name-only is simpler and more predictable. Broader search is more powerful but can produce surprising results. Jean should weigh in.
Sort Dropdown

Defaults to Alphabetical A to Z. This matches how Jean organizes her catalog and how daylily buyers are used to browsing. Other options: Price low to high, Price high to low, and Newest First (by year introduced or the date Jean added the plant to the catalog).

Filter Sidebar (left column)

Seven filter categories matching the plant data in the database: Color, Bloom Size, Ploidy, Foliage, Bloom Time, Rebloomer, and Price Range. Filters work together: checking "Orange/Gold" and "Rebloomer only" shows only orange/gold rebloomers. The count next to each option (e.g., "Orange / Gold (11)") shows how many plants match that value. "Clear all" at the top removes all active filters at once.

Decision needed: Should the filter counts in parentheses be included? They are helpful but add some development complexity. If Jean does not want them, they are easy to drop. Decision needed: Should filter sections be collapsible (clicking the label hides/shows the options)? The chevron arrows suggest they can collapse. This helps on pages with many plants. Or we can leave all sections always open.
Plant Grid (3 columns, 12 cards shown)

Each card shows a photo placeholder, plant name, a key-attributes line (ploidy, bloom time, scape height), a short description, the price, and a "View Details" button. The alternating slightly darker grey on the photo placeholders is just to distinguish cards visually in the wireframe; real photos will look completely different. Sold-out plants (Catherine Woodbery) show a grey badge on the photo, grayed price, and a disabled "Currently Unavailable" button.

Decision needed: 3 columns vs 4 columns. Three columns leaves more room for the short description on each card, which helps customers browse without clicking into every detail page. Four columns fits more plants per row. The homepage used 4 columns; the catalog could stay at 3 for readability, or match the homepage at 4. Jean's call. Decision needed: Should sold-out plants appear at all in the catalog, or be filtered out automatically? Showing them lets customers see the full range and contact Jean if interested. Hiding them keeps the catalog clean. Jean should decide what she prefers.
Attribute Line on Each Card

The small grey text under each plant name ("Tetraploid • Midseason • 28 inch scape") gives experienced daylily buyers the most important facts at a glance. Casual buyers can ignore it. These pull directly from the plant's database record and cost nothing extra to display.

Pagination

Shows 12 plants per page with numbered navigation at the bottom. Page 1 is active in this wireframe. With 48 total plants the example shows 8 pages at 12 per page. The dots (...) compress middle pages when there are many.

Decision needed: How many plants per page? 12 is easy to browse, 24 reduces page-clicking, and "show all" is how PlantFans works (everything on one page, scroll down). More plants per page means a heavier page load on mobile. Jean should decide what her customers are used to.
Cart Count (top right, showing "2")

Shown as 2 here to demonstrate the live count. In real use it starts at 0 and increases as plants are added. It updates without reloading the page.

Mobile Behavior (not shown)

On phones and small tablets, the filter sidebar collapses. A "Filter" button appears above the grid; tapping it opens the filters in a slide-in panel. The 3-column grid drops to 1 or 2 columns. This is standard behavior and will be built during development.
