Ⓒ When We Ship

Daylilies are shipped as bare-root divisions, meaning the plants arrive without soil. This is the safest way to ship live plants over long distances and gives them the best chance of thriving in your garden.

Jean ships in spring and fall when temperatures are mild enough for safe transit. Orders placed during the shipping season are typically packed and shipped within a few days. Orders placed outside the shipping window are held and shipped when conditions are right.

Jean will email you personally when your order ships with the tracking number and any specific care notes for your plants.

⚐ State Shipping Restrictions

Most states in the continental US can receive live plant shipments without any issues. However, some states have agricultural import regulations that affect how or whether we can ship to you.

We cannot ship to these states or territories: California, Hawaii, Alaska, Puerto Rico, and other US territories. These destinations have strict agricultural import rules that prevent us from shipping live plants. We're sorry for the inconvenience.

☸ Caring for Your Plants When They Arrive

Bare-root daylilies are resilient travelers, but they do need a little attention when they arrive. Here is what to do:

  1. 1
    Open the box right away. Do not leave plants sealed in the shipping box for more than a day. They need air circulation.
  2. 2
    Inspect your plants. Bare-root daylilies may look rough after shipping. This is normal. The foliage may be wilted or trimmed, but the crown and roots are what matter. If anything looks wrong, email Jean right away.
  3. 3
    Soak the roots before planting. Place the bare roots in a bucket of water for 1 to 2 hours before planting. This rehydrates the plant and gives it a head start.
  4. 4
    Plant as soon as possible. Daylilies do best when planted within a day or two of arrival. Choose a spot with at least 6 hours of sun and well-drained soil. Plant with the crown just at or slightly below soil level.
  5. 5
    Water thoroughly after planting. Keep the soil consistently moist for the first two to three weeks while the plant establishes. After that, daylilies are quite drought-tolerant.
  6. 6
    Be patient. Newly planted daylilies may not bloom the first season. They are putting their energy into establishing roots. Most will bloom beautifully in their second season and beyond.
