Flow Diagram Annotations: Customer Journey

Notes explaining the key paths, decisions, and business rules illustrated in the diagram above.

The Main Path (blue arrows, left-center column)

The primary customer journey runs straight down the center: arrive at the homepage, browse the catalog, view a plant detail, add to cart, proceed to checkout, enter info, pay, receive confirmation. This is the happy path and the one that most customers will follow. Every step links to a corresponding wireframe.

Browsing Loop (grey dashed arrows, left side)

Customers rarely convert on the first plant they see. The grey dashed arrows on the left show the natural browsing loop: from the catalog back to more catalog pages, and from a plant detail page back to the catalog. The "Continue Shopping" button in the cart also feeds back into this loop. This is expected behavior and the site is designed to support it without losing cart contents.

Coordinated Flowers Loop (right side, at Plant Detail)

On the Plant Detail page, customers may click a Coordinated Flower and navigate to that plant's detail page. This can create a browsing chain through multiple plant pages before the customer adds anything to cart. The loop is intentional and desirable: it increases the likelihood of multi-plant orders.

Blocked State (red branch at Checkout)

When a customer enters a shipping address in California, Hawaii, Alaska, Puerto Rico, or a US territory, the payment section is hidden and they cannot proceed. This is a hard stop. The cart remains intact so if they have a different address they can re-enter it.

Business rule: Blocked state = no order possible. Payment form does not load. No order record is created.
Flagged State (amber dashed branch at Checkout)

When a customer enters a Texas, Arizona, or Florida address, a notice appears explaining agricultural regulations. They must actively click "I understand" before the payment section unlocks. If they accept, they rejoin the main payment flow. If they click "Email Jean instead," they are directed to contact Jean directly and do not complete the order online. The order record, if it is completed, carries a flag that Jean sees in her admin.

Business rule: Flagged state orders can proceed if the customer accepts the notice. Jean reviews these orders before shipping.
Payment Failure (red dashed branch at Payment)

If Square declines the card, an error message appears inline on the checkout page and the customer can try again with corrected details or a different card. No order record is created on a failed payment. The customer stays on the checkout page with their form data intact.

Automatic Confirmation Email (green branch at Order Confirmation)

The moment an order is successfully placed, the system sends a confirmation email to the customer automatically. Jean does not need to do anything. The email contains the order number, plant list, totals, and a message from Jean. Tracking information is not included because Jean sends that personally when she ships.

Order Status Page (bottom of diagram)

Customers can return to the Order Status page at any time using their order number and email address. Three possible status states are shown: Received or Processing (waiting for Jean to pack and ship), Shipped (tracking number visible), and Delivered. Jean manually advances the status in her admin; the system does not track delivery automatically.
