Page Annotations: Bundles Page (Wireframe 03c — New)

A new standalone catalog page at /bundles listing every active bundle Jean has configured, browsable independently of any individual plant page. Two states shown: the populated grid and the empty state shown before Jean has configured any bundles. Plain English throughout.

Navigation Tab: "Bundles"

A fifth tab added to the top navigation bar on all public pages, positioned after the four plant-type tabs and before the cart. It is styled in green (rather than the standard blue) to signal that bundles are a distinct kind of page: not a category of plant type, but a curated purchase option. Like the other tabs, it can be toggled on or off from the admin Settings page so Jean can hide it when no bundles are configured and show it once she has at least one ready.

Jean-flagged: Should this page be called "Bundles," "Collections," "Garden Collections," "Plant Sets," or something else? "Bundles" is used here as the default. Jean should confirm or rename before the build is finalized. Decision needed: Should the Bundles tab be added to the admin Settings panel as a toggle (like the other four plant-type tabs), so Jean controls when it appears? Recommended: yes. It should only show when at least one bundle is active. Jean can also manually hide it during the off-season.
Bundle Card: Photo Strip

Each card shows a horizontal strip of small photo thumbnails, one per plant in the bundle, side by side. A 3-plant bundle shows 3 photos; a 9-plant bundle shows 9 very narrow slivers. This gives an immediate visual impression of the color range in the set. A green savings badge overlays the top-right corner of the photo strip showing the dollar amount saved vs. buying individually.

Decision needed: For 7 and 9-plant bundles the photo slivers become very narrow. Should the card cap the photo strip at 5 visible photos with a "+N more" indicator for larger bundles, or always show all photos regardless of count? Capping at 5 keeps cards visually consistent. Always showing all is more informative but messier for large sets.
Bundle Card: Size Label

A small colored pill at the top of each card body shows the bundle size: 3-Plant (green), 5-Plant (blue), 7-Plant (purple), 9-Plant (amber). Color-coded by size so customers can scan the grid and instantly spot the bundle size they are interested in without reading every card. This also helps customers who are filtering by size see that their filter is working.

Bundle Card: Plant Name Chips

Each plant in the bundle is shown as a small grey chip below the description. For large bundles (7 or 9 plants) this creates a longer list of chips but keeps the card honest: customers know exactly what they are buying. Plant names in chips are not links; the full plant detail is accessible from the bundle detail page (Wireframe 03b).

Bundle Card: Anchor Plant Reference

A small note at the bottom of each card says "Curated with [Plant Name]" with a link to that plant's detail page. This connects the bundle back to its origin in the data model (each bundle is anchored to one plant in the plant_coordinations table) and lets customers navigate to the full plant detail if they want more information before deciding.

Decision needed: Should the anchor plant reference be shown at all on this page? It is a useful navigation aid but it also exposes the internal data structure in a way that may feel slightly odd ("Curated with Acapulco Sunset" raises the question of why that plant specifically). An alternative is to simply omit it here and keep it only on the plant detail page. Jean's preference.
"View Bundle and Add to Cart" Button

Navigates to the bundle detail page (Wireframe 03b) where the customer sees the full plant list and confirms the purchase before adding to cart. The button does not add the bundle to cart directly from this page: the intermediate bundle detail page gives customers the chance to read about each plant and confirm the set is right for their garden before committing. This is especially important for the larger 7 and 9-plant bundles where the purchase decision is more significant.

Decision needed: Should there also be a direct "Add to Cart" option on this page for customers who have already visited the bundle detail page and are returning to purchase? A returning customer who knows what they want may find the extra click frustrating. One option: show "Add to Cart" directly on the card with a smaller "View Details" link below it. Jean's preference on which interaction model she wants.
Filters: Bundle Size and Plant Type

Two filter dropdowns in the toolbar: Bundle Size (All, 3 plants, 5 plants, 7 plants, 9 plants) and Plant Type (All types, Daylily bundles, Iris bundles, Mixed bundles). At launch with only daylily bundles configured, the Plant Type filter will show only "Daylily bundles" as a meaningful option, but the dropdown is built generically so it remains useful as Jean adds other plant types. The result count in the title bar updates as filters are applied.

Sort Options

Five sort options: Savings most first (default, surfaces the best deals), Price low to high, Price high to low, Bundle Size, and Alphabetical. Defaulting to savings-first encourages customers to see the most compelling bundles first and reinforces the value proposition of the page.

Empty State (second variation)

When Jean has not configured any bundles or has turned the bundle toggle off on all plants, the page shows a friendly explanation of what bundles are and a note that they are coming soon. A "Browse the Daylily Catalog" button gives customers a clear next action. This state is also what customers see if Jean hides all bundles at the start of the off-season before configuring the new season's sets.

Stock Handling on the Grid

If any plant in a bundle is sold out, the bundle card should show a "Partially unavailable" indicator and the "View Bundle and Add to Cart" button should be replaced with a disabled "Currently unavailable" button. The card remains visible on the page so customers can see the bundle exists and contact Jean if interested. Full removal from the grid is controlled by the bundle_enabled toggle in the admin Plant Editor.

URL Structure

The page lives at /bundles. Individual bundle detail pages live at /daylilies/[slug]/bundle (Wireframe 03b). The Bundles page links to bundle detail pages; bundle detail pages link back to the Bundles page via the breadcrumb as well as to the individual anchor plant. This creates a complete navigation triangle: Bundles page → Bundle detail → Plant detail → back to Catalog or Bundles page.

Admin Settings Change Required

The Settings page (Wireframe 15) needs a new toggle in the Plant Tabs section: "Bundles tab." This controls whether the Bundles tab appears in the top navigation. Jean turns it on once she has at least one bundle configured and ready to sell. The toggle should behave identically to the existing Daylilies, Iris, Lilies, and Other Perennials toggles.
