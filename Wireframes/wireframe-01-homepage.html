About Jean and Dazzling Daylilies

Hi, I'm Jean, and I've been growing daylilies in Fairfax, Iowa for over twenty years. What started as a few plants along the fence has grown into a garden full of hundreds of varieties in every color imaginable. Every plant I sell has been grown right here in my own yard, and I dig and ship them fresh when the season is right.

I love connecting with other gardeners and helping people find the perfect plant for their space. If you have a question about a variety, a tricky spot in your yard, or you just want to talk daylilies, feel free to reach out. I'm always happy to chat.

Questions? Email me at dazzlingdaylilies@yahoo.com.

Page Annotations: Homepage (Wireframe 01 of 17)

These notes explain what each region does, what triggers each action, and what decisions this layout commits us to. Plain English for Jean.

Cookie Banner (dark strip at the very top)

Shows up once when a customer first visits. It tells them the site only uses essential cookies to make the shopping cart work, nothing else. They click "Got it" and it goes away permanently. No complicated checkboxes or consent flows needed. This is just basic legal hygiene for a US-only site.

Site Header (business name and tagline)

The name "Dazzling Daylilies" is the main heading. The tagline below it is placeholder copy; Jean should approve or rewrite it. This area will also hold a logo graphic if Jean wants one designed. For now it is just text.

Content needed: Jean to approve or rewrite tagline
Navigation Tabs (Daylilies, Iris, Lilies, Other Perennials)

These four tabs match Jean's catalog categories. At launch, only Daylilies is clickable. The other three show up grayed out with a "(coming soon)" note so visitors know more is coming. When Jean is ready to add irises or lilies, she turns them on in the admin Settings page and they become active automatically, no developer needed.

Decision needed: Should inactive tabs show as grayed-out (as shown here) or be hidden entirely until active? Grayed = signals future categories. Hidden = cleaner but gives no preview.
Cart Button (top right)

Shows how many items are in the customer's cart. Clicking it takes them to the Cart page. The number updates live as they add plants. This appears on every page of the site.

Hero Section (large banner with text overlay)

A big, welcoming photo fills this area. The white box over it contains a short introduction and a button that takes customers directly to the catalog. The white box keeps the text readable no matter what the photo looks like behind it.

Content needed: Jean to provide 1 to 3 candidate garden or bloom photos for this area. Placeholder copy should be reviewed and rewritten in her voice.
Featured Plants (4 cards in a row)

Shows four hand-picked plants. Jean (or Edward) controls which plants appear here from the admin panel. On phones and small tablets the cards stack vertically instead of sitting side by side. One card shows the "Sold Out" state so Jean can see how unavailable plants look: gray badge, grayed price, and a disabled button that says "Currently Unavailable."

Decision needed: Should sold-out plants still appear on the homepage (as shown) or be filtered out automatically? Showing them keeps the grid full. Hiding them means the grid may look sparse when stock is low.
"See All Daylilies" Button

Takes the customer to the full catalog page. It is styled as a secondary (outlined) button rather than the main blue button because the homepage is for browsing and warming up, not an immediate hard sell.

About Jean Section

A photo of Jean or her garden on the left, with a few paragraphs of personal introduction on the right. This is one of the most important sections on the homepage for a small-batch grower because customers buy from a person, not a company. The text shown is placeholder; Jean should write her own bio or Edward can draft it for her to edit.

Content needed: Jean's photo (or a garden photo) and a short bio (3 to 5 sentences is plenty).
Footer: Three Columns

Left column: contact info (email address; physical address omitted unless Jean wants it public). Middle column: links to Shipping Information, Order Status Lookup, Privacy Policy, and Terms of Service. Right column: email newsletter signup connected to Mailchimp.

Footer: Email Signup (Mailchimp)

Customers type their email and click Subscribe. It goes directly into Jean's Mailchimp contact list. Jean can then send seasonal emails: new variety announcements, shipping window reminders, or garden tips. Customers only get added if they sign up here; no emails are sent without their permission. This requires a Mailchimp account and an API key, which gets entered in the admin Settings page once during setup.

Page Width and Mobile Behavior

The content is contained within a 1,160-pixel-wide column, centered on screen. On wide monitors there will be white space on the sides. On phones, the 4-column plant grid collapses to 1 or 2 columns, and the About section stacks vertically (photo on top, text below). Everything is readable without zooming or sideways scrolling.

Decision needed: 1,160px is a standard e-commerce width, similar to what PlantFans uses. Happy to adjust if Jean prefers a wider or narrower feel before coding begins.
