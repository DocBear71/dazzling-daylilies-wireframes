Upload Photos

Upload plant photos in bulk. Photos are automatically matched to plants based on their filename. Name your files using the plant's URL slug (shown in the Plants list) followed by an underscore and a number.

How to Name Your Photo Files

The filename must start with the plant's URL slug, then an underscore, then a number. The slug for each plant is shown in the Plants list under the plant name. Capitalization does not matter. JPEG and PNG are both accepted.

Examples of correct filenames:

acapulco-sunset_1.jpg acapulco-sunset_2.jpg strawberry-candy_1.jpg happy-returns_1.jpg morning-mist_3.JPG

Examples of filenames that will NOT match:

AcapulcoSunset.jpg acapulco sunset 1.jpg IMG_4582.jpg photo1.jpg

Page Annotations: Admin Photo Uploader (Wireframe 12 of 17)

Three states shown: the empty drop zone, the upload-in-progress view, and the review-and-confirm table. This page handles the bulk photo workflow Jean will use at the start of every season. Plain English throughout.

Naming Convention Help Box

Shown prominently at the top of the page, every time Jean visits. It gives her the exact pattern to follow with working examples and explicit examples of filenames that will not work. This box is the first line of defense against upload confusion. Jean should read it carefully the first time she uploads photos and keep it in mind when naming files on her computer before uploading.

Business rule: The slug is the photo-matching key. A filename of "acapulco-sunset_1.jpg" matches the plant whose slug is "acapulco-sunset." The number after the underscore sets the display order of photos within a plant. Filenames that do not follow this pattern cannot be auto-matched and must be assigned manually in the review step.
State 1: Drop Zone

The default view when Jean opens the Photos page. She can drag photos from her computer directly onto the grey dashed area, or click "Browse Files" to use the standard file picker dialog. Both methods accept up to 100 photos at once. JPEG and PNG are accepted. Files over 20 MB are rejected with an error message before upload begins.

Decision needed: The 20 MB per-file limit is a reasonable default for high-resolution camera photos, but Jean should confirm her photos are typically under this size. If she shoots in RAW and exports large JPEGs, this limit may need adjusting. The limit exists because of Hostinger's shared hosting constraints on upload size and processing time.
State 2: Upload in Progress

Each file shows its filename, a progress bar, and a status label: Done (green checkmark), a percentage for files currently uploading, Waiting for files in the queue, or "No match" for files that uploaded successfully but whose filename did not match any plant slug. The "No match" status in the progress view is informational only; those files are not discarded yet and will appear in the review table where Jean can assign them manually.

Business rule: On Hostinger shared hosting, long-running processes are not reliable. Photo uploads are handled as standard HTTP file uploads, not background jobs. WebP conversion happens after the upload completes. For a batch of 100 photos, this may take a noticeable amount of time. Jean should not close the browser tab during this process.
State 3: Review Table

After all files finish uploading, Jean sees a table with every uploaded photo. Each row shows a small preview thumbnail, the filename, a match status indicator, and the assigned plant. Three match states are shown in this wireframe: green "Matched" (filename matched a plant slug automatically), amber "Partial" (filename partially matched but needs confirmation), and red "No match" (could not be matched at all).

Reassign Link and Dropdown

Every row, even correctly matched ones, has a "Reassign" link in case Jean wants to redirect a photo to a different plant. Clicking it replaces the plant name with a dropdown of all plants. The amber "Partial" row (happy-returns_extra.jpg) shows the dropdown already open. Unmatched rows (IMG_4582.jpg, garden-wide-shot.jpg) start with the dropdown open and a "Select a plant" placeholder so Jean can see immediately that action is needed.

Decision needed: Should unmatched photos be discarded automatically when Jean clicks "Confirm and Save," or should saving be blocked until every unmatched photo is either assigned or explicitly discarded? Blocking saves is safer but more friction. Auto-discarding is faster but Jean may not notice she lost a photo. The wireframe shows a note at the bottom that unassigned photos are auto-discarded; Jean should confirm she is comfortable with this behavior.
Confirm and Save Button

After Jean reviews the table, she clicks "Confirm and Save Matched Photos." This triggers three things: matched and assigned photos are attached to their respective plant records, each photo is converted to WebP in three sizes (thumbnail 300x300, medium 800x800, large 1600x1600), and the originals are retained in storage. Unmatched and unassigned photos are discarded. The process may take a few seconds for large batches.

Business rule: Photo conversion to WebP uses Spatie Laravel Media Library or a custom service. Three sizes are generated per photo. Original files are kept. The thumbnail is the version used in catalog cards, the cart, the order sheet, and the confirmation email. The large version is used on the plant detail page.
Discard Button Per Row

Jean can discard any individual photo from the review table by clicking the red "Discard" button on that row. The row is removed from the table immediately. Discarded photos are not saved to any plant record. This is useful if Jean accidentally included an out-of-focus photo or a non-plant image in the batch.

Summary Bar Above the Confirm Button

Shows three counts at a glance: matched automatically (green), partial matches needing review (amber), and unmatched photos (red). Jean can see the overall state of the batch before committing. If the red count is non-zero, she should scroll through the table and either assign or discard those photos before saving.

WebP Conversion Note

A short note at the bottom of the page reminds Jean that her photos will be converted automatically. She does not need to do anything special; the system handles the conversion. The originals are kept in case re-conversion is needed. Jean never interacts with the WebP files directly.
