Page Annotations: Admin Plants List (Wireframe 10 of 17)

This is where Jean manages her full plant catalog: searching, filtering, adjusting stock status, toggling visibility, and navigating to the plant editor. Plain English throughout.

Search Box

Filters the plant list by name as Jean types. Searches the plant name field only. Useful when Jean wants to find a specific plant quickly without scrolling through all 48.

Filter Dropdowns (Type, Stock, Visibility)

Three independent filters Jean can combine. Type filters by plant category (Daylily, Iris, Lily, Other Perennial). Stock shows all plants, only in-stock, or only sold-out. Visibility shows all plants, only active ones visible to customers, or only inactive ones currently hidden. The result count in the toolbar updates as filters are applied.

Decision needed: Should filtering and searching happen instantly as Jean types and selects (live filtering, no button press), or should there be a "Search" button to trigger the update? Live filtering is more fluid but requires Livewire to handle the reactivity. A button is simpler to build. Given that this is an admin tool Jean uses frequently, live filtering is the better experience if it is feasible.
Bulk Actions Bar

Appears when Jean checks one or more rows using the checkboxes. Shows how many plants are selected and a dropdown with available bulk actions: Mark as In Stock, Mark as Sold Out, Set Active, Set Inactive. Jean picks the action and clicks Apply. The bar disappears when she cancels or after the action completes. This wireframe shows 3 plants selected (rows for Bela Lugosi, Catherine Woodbery, and Heavenly Angel Ice are checked). This is the fastest way to flip stock status on multiple plants at once after a dig.

Business rule: Bulk delete is intentionally not offered here. Individual plants can only be deleted from inside the Plant Editor, which requires a confirmation step. This prevents Jean from accidentally mass-deleting her catalog.
Plant Thumbnail

The first photo associated with the plant, shown as a small square. If no photos have been uploaded yet, the cell shows a grey "No photo" placeholder (see Heavenly Angel Ice, row 9). The thumbnail is purely visual and not clickable; the Edit button is the action link.

Plant Name and Slug

The display name is shown in bold. Below it, in smaller monospace text, is the plant's slug (e.g., "acapulco-sunset"). The slug is shown here as a reference for Jean when naming photo files for upload. She doesn't need to edit it directly on this screen; it is set when the plant is created and can be changed in the Plant Editor.

Active Toggle (on/off switch)

A green toggle means the plant is active and visible to customers on the public catalog. A grey toggle means the plant is inactive and hidden from customers entirely, even if it is in stock. Jean can flip this toggle directly in the table without opening the Plant Editor. Inactive rows appear slightly faded (see Cosmic Hummingbird, row 6) so they are visually distinct from active plants.

Business rule: In Stock and Active are two separate flags. A plant can be In Stock but Inactive (hidden from customers), or Sold Out but Active (visible with a sold-out badge). Jean controls both independently. Decision needed: Should toggling Active directly in the table take effect immediately (one click, no confirmation), or should it ask "Are you sure?" first? Immediate is faster for bulk work. A confirmation prevents accidental toggles. Given that toggling is reversible with another click, immediate is probably fine. Jean should confirm her preference.
Photo Count Column

Shows how many photos are attached to each plant. Plants with zero photos are flagged in red ("No photos") because a plant with no photo will look broken on the public catalog. The Photos button in the action cell for zero-photo plants is styled in blue to draw attention to the gap.

Edit and Photos Buttons

Edit opens the Plant Editor for that row. Photos opens the photo management view for that plant specifically (a shortcut that bypasses the full Photo Uploader). Both are small grey buttons to keep the table visually light.

Table Footer: Summary Stats and Pagination

The footer shows a compact summary of the full catalog: 48 plants total, 7 sold out, 1 inactive. These counts reflect the unfiltered catalog, not the current filtered view, so Jean always knows the overall state of her catalog regardless of which filter is active. Pagination shows 15 plants per page.

Decision needed: 15 plants per page is shown here. With 48 plants that is 4 pages. Would Jean prefer 20 or 25 per page to reduce clicking? Or "show all" since she is managing her own catalog and likely wants to see everything at once? Jean's call.
Column Sorting

Column headers are clickable to sort the table. The current sort is by plant name A to Z (the up arrow on the Plant column header indicates ascending sort). Clicking the same column again sorts descending. Clicking another column sorts by that column. Sortable columns: Plant name, Price. Status columns (Type, Stock, Active) are filterable via the dropdowns above but not sortable as columns.
