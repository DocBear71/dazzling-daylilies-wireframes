Page Annotations: Checkout Page (Wireframe 05 of 17)

Two states shown: standard checkout (top) and blocked state (bottom). The flagged-state notice is visible in the standard checkout to illustrate where and how it appears. Plain English throughout.

"Guest checkout — no account required" Note

Shown in the page title bar, right at the top. Sets the expectation immediately: customers will not be asked to create a password or log in at any point. Per contract, guest checkout is the only option for this site.

Section Numbers (1, 2, 3)

The form is divided into three numbered sections: Contact Information, Shipping Address, and Payment. The numbered circles make the form feel shorter and less daunting. Customers can see at a glance that there are only three steps before they are done.

Section 1: Contact Information

Full Name and Email Address are required. Phone is optional. The email address is used to send the automatic order confirmation and is how customers look up their order on the Order Status page later. Jean can also reach customers by email if there is a shipping question.

Section 2: Shipping Address

Standard address fields. State is a dropdown rather than a free-text field so the restricted-state logic can fire reliably. ZIP code is a text field rather than a number field to handle leading zeros (New England ZIP codes like 01001 would lose their leading zero in a numeric input).

Business rule: The state dropdown is where restricted-state checking happens. As soon as a customer selects a state, the appropriate notice appears or disappears dynamically. No page reload needed.
Flagged State Notice (yellow box, TX / CA / AZ / FL)

Appears automatically when the customer selects Texas, California, Arizona, or Florida. It explains that agricultural regulations apply, tells them Jean will follow up before shipping, and offers two choices: "I understand, continue to payment" or "Email Jean instead." The customer must actively click one of these buttons before the payment section becomes available. This ensures Jean has a record that the notice was shown and accepted, satisfying the business rule.

Business rule: For flagged states, the order CAN proceed if the customer clicks "I understand." Jean reviews these orders before shipping. The order record stores a flag (restricted_state_notice_shown = true) so Jean knows which orders need extra review. Decision needed: The current copy says Jean will "contact you before shipping to confirm." Should this be stronger or softer? Jean should review and approve the exact wording since it is a legal/business commitment.
Blocked State Notice (red box, HI / AK / PR and territories)

Appears when the customer selects Hawaii, Alaska, Puerto Rico, or a US territory. The payment section is hidden entirely and the Place Order button is disabled. The customer literally cannot complete the order. A clear explanation and Jean's email address are shown. The cart remains intact so if the customer has a different shipping address, they can change the state and proceed.

Business rule: Blocked states are a hard stop. No workaround, no exceptions. The payment form does not load and no order record is created.
Section 3: Payment (Square Widget)

Square's payment form is embedded here as a hosted widget. The customer enters their card number, expiration date, and CVV directly into Square's secure fields. Card data never passes through Dazzling Daylilies' server at any point. Square handles all PCI compliance. Only credit and debit cards are accepted: no PayPal, no Afterpay, no buy-now-pay-later per the contract.

Business rule: Square credit card only. No Afterpay or buy-now-pay-later options will be offered.
"Place Order" Button

The final action. Submitting triggers three things in sequence: Square processes the payment, the order record is created in the database, and a confirmation email is sent to the customer. If payment fails, the customer sees an error message and the form stays open so they can try again or use a different card. No order record is created on a failed payment.

Order Summary Panel (right column)

Shows a compact version of the cart with mini thumbnails, quantities, subtotal, shipping, and total. Matches the cart summary from the previous page so there are no surprises. An "Edit cart" link lets customers go back and change their order without losing what they have typed in the form.

Decision needed: Should the order summary panel be sticky (stays visible as the customer scrolls down the long form)? On a desktop screen this is helpful because the total stays in view while filling in address and payment. This is shown as sticky in this wireframe (position: sticky). Jean should confirm this feels right or if she prefers it to scroll with the page.
Terms and Privacy Note Below Place Order Button

A single line stating that by placing the order the customer agrees to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, with links to both. This is standard e-commerce legal practice and reduces Jean's exposure. The links open in a new tab so the customer does not lose their filled-out form.
